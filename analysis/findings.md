Key Economic Findings

1. Trade Dynamics

Uzbekistan’s foreign trade expanded substantially between 2015 and 2025. Exports increased from approximately $12.5 billion in 2015 to $34.2 billion in 2025, while imports rose from approximately $12.4 billion to $48.5 billion over the same period.

Export growth was uneven, with periods of decline and acceleration. Exports fell in 2016 and 2020, but recorded particularly strong growth in 2023 and 2025. Imports followed a similar pattern but generally grew faster than exports over the period.

Overall, the data indicate a significant expansion in Uzbekistan’s integration into international trade, accompanied by a widening gap between import and export values.

2. Trade Balance

Uzbekistan’s trade balance changed from a small surplus in 2015 to a persistent trade deficit in the following years. The balance was approximately +$91 million in 2015, but turned negative in 2016 and reached around -$13.8 billion in 2023.

The deficit narrowed slightly in 2024 to approximately -$12.7 billion, before widening again to -$14.3 billion in 2025. This reflects the fact that imports have consistently exceeded exports in recent years.

The widening trade deficit occurred alongside strong growth in total trade, indicating that Uzbekistan’s increasing participation in international trade has been accompanied by a substantial increase in import demand.

3. Export Product Structure

Uzbekistan’s export composition changed considerably between 2015 and 2025. The share of mineral fuels, lubricating oils and related products declined from approximately 21.5% of exports in 2015 to 4.3% in 2025.

At the same time, services increased their share from approximately 24.5% to 29.8%, while other goods rose from approximately 15.4% to 29.5%. The share of machinery and transport equipment also increased, although it remained relatively modest, rising from approximately 1.1% to 3.4%.

These changes indicate a substantial shift in the composition of Uzbekistan’s exports over the period. However, the broad product categories used in the dataset mean that the results should be interpreted as changes in the overall export structure rather than evidence of detailed technological diversification.

4. Export Destination Structure

Uzbekistan’s export destination structure changed between 2015 and 2025. The share of exports going to China declined from approximately 19.8% in 2015 to 7.3% in 2025, while Kazakhstan’s share fell from approximately 14.8% to 4.8%.

Russia remained an important export destination, although its share changed relatively little, declining from approximately 14.6% to 13.3%. At the same time, several other destinations increased their shares, including Afghanistan, France, the United Arab Emirates, Kyrgyzstan, Tajikistan and the United States.

The changes suggest that Uzbekistan’s export geography became less concentrated among its largest individual destinations over the period, with a broader distribution of exports across international markets.

5. Export Concentration

The Herfindahl–Hirschman Index (HHI) shows different patterns in Uzbekistan’s export concentration across products and destination markets.

Partner concentration decreased substantially. Export partner HHI fell from 1,562.93 in 2015 to 1,037.58 in 2025, a decline of approximately 33.6%. This indicates that exports became less concentrated among individual destination markets.

Product concentration remained higher and more volatile. Product HHI increased from 1,870.56 in 2015 to 2,377.55 in 2025, with a peak of 2,676.48 in 2020. It subsequently declined in 2022 before increasing again in 2023 and remaining relatively high through 2025.

Overall, the results suggest that changes in Uzbekistan’s export geography were more pronounced than changes in its product composition. While destination-market concentration decreased, product concentration remained comparatively high and volatile during the period.

6. Overall Assessment

Between 2015 and 2025, Uzbekistan experienced substantial growth in the scale of its international trade. Both exports and imports increased significantly, while imports generally grew faster than exports, resulting in a persistent trade deficit.

At the same time, the structure of Uzbekistan’s exports changed. The share of mineral fuels decreased considerably, while services and other goods became more important. Export destinations also became less concentrated, as the shares of several major individual markets declined while a wider range of destinations gained importance.

The HHI results reinforce this distinction: export geography became less concentrated, while product concentration remained relatively high and volatile. This suggests that Uzbekistan’s trade transformation during the period was characterized not only by expanding trade volumes, but also by significant changes in the geographical distribution of exports and a more limited change in the concentration of the product structure.

Overall, the findings provide evidence of a changing export profile and expanding international trade integration, while also highlighting the continued importance of product-level concentration and the trade deficit as areas for further analysis.
